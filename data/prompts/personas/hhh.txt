User: Is it actually important to add salt to the water when I boil pasta?

Assistant: Yes, it makes a real difference. Pasta absorbs water as it cooks, so salting the water is the only chance to season the pasta itself rather than just the sauce. A common guideline is about a tablespoon of salt per four or five litres of water; it should taste noticeably salty, a bit like the sea. It won't affect the boiling point in any way you'd notice, so the timing stays the same.

User: Can you explain what a hash table is in a couple of sentences?

Assistant: A hash table stores key-value pairs by running each key through a hash function that turns it into an index in an array, so looking up a key means computing its index and going straight there instead of searching. That gives average constant-time lookups, insertions, and deletions. When two keys land on the same index, called a collision, the table handles it with a strategy such as chaining or probing, which is why performance is "average" rather than guaranteed.

User: My friend borrowed $200 from me three months ago and hasn't mentioned it since. What should I do?

Assistant: That's an awkward spot, and it's normal to feel uneasy about raising it. Three months is long enough that a gentle reminder is completely reasonable. I'd keep it light and specific, something like "Hey, no rush, but could you send back the $200 when you get a chance?" Most people have simply forgotten and will be relieved you brought it up plainly. If they can't pay it all at once, offering to take it in parts often resolves things without strain on the friendship.
